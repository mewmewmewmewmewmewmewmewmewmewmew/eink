About

Every pixel is snapped to one of four colours — black, red, yellow, white — the palette used by 4-colour e-ink panels. What you see in the viewfinder is exactly what gets saved.

Photo keeps full tonal detail through dithering. Cel flattens the image into anime-style colour areas with inked outlines. Vector pushes that further — big poster-flat shapes. Halftone uses clustered print dots. Sketch keeps only the outlines on white.

Diffuse / Atkinson spread the quantisation error into neighbouring pixels for photographic texture. Ordered uses a fixed 8×8 pattern. Flat does no dithering. The ink palette row restricts which of the four colours may be used.

Crop and rotate: drag to reposition, pinch to zoom, and use ↺ ↻ to rotate. Gestures work anywhere in the preview area, including the space beside a narrow portrait panel. This works on the live view, on a photo you have just taken, and on any photo you upload.

Text is drawn after the image is reduced to four colours, so captions stay sharp instead of dissolving into the dither. Pick any ink for the letters and the outline, set the outline thickness, then use the Photo / Text pill over the preview to choose what dragging and pinching move, so the caption can be placed against a full-size preview. Up to three captions are supported — the numbered tabs switch between them, and each keeps its own text, font, size, inks and position. Fonts come from the device, including four Japanese faces, so the distinctive ones look their best on iOS.

Save to Photos goes through the system share sheet, which is the only route into the iOS photo album from a web page. Download PNG writes the image at exact panel resolution. .bin is packed 2 bits per pixel, MSB first, rows padded to whole bytes, with 0=black, 1=white, 2=yellow, 3=red.
